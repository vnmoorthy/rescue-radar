02 · The problem

When the phones jam,
the pleas go public

  1. 01

    Emergency lines saturate

    In a major disaster, call volume outruns 911 and emergency lines. Many callers never get through.

  2. 02

    Pleas move to social media

    People post wherever someone might see it: the street, the water level, who is trapped and how long they have.

  3. 03

    Nobody can read it all

    Rescue requests are a thin slice of a firehose of news, prayers and opinion. No team can triage millions of posts in time.

10 · Stack, safeguards, limits

Built to be trusted,
honest about where it fails

Sponsor stack

FireworksLoRA fine-tuning on serverless training, plus serving the checkpoint
NVIDIANemotron 3.5 Super: the open-weight base model we adapt
MicrosoftPresidio PII redaction before human review; Foundry deployment is a next step, not built yet

Responsible AI

  • Human in the loop. The model ranks; a person dispatches.
  • Privacy first. Best-effort PII redaction before a dispatcher reads a post.
  • Open base model. The LoRA adapter could be exported for self-hosting (not done yet).
  • Honest evaluation. Unseen disasters, one shared prompt, confidence intervals and significance tests.

Limitations

  • It misses some. Rescue-critical recall is pending, so it runs beside 911, never instead of it.
  • Weakest category: pending.
  • English tweets, 2016 to 2019. Other languages and platforms are not yet tested.
  • Labels are crowd-annotated. Some ground truth is itself ambiguous.
11 · The company

Rescue Radar as a company

Who pays

  • 911 centers and state emergency management

Product

  • Dispatch console: feed, map and priority queue
  • API that screens posts for an agency's own tools
  • Private deployment in the agency's own cloud: planned, not built

Pricing model

  • Annual subscription per agency, budgeted before the storm
  • Usage tier for high-volume screening in a surge
  • No price list yet: the pilots will set it
